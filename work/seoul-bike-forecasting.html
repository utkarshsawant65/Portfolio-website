Forecasting hourly bike demand, and catching a leaked benchmark

M.Sc. thesis comparing seven deep architectures for hourly demand forecasting, reaching 88.83% R² and correcting a data-leakage flaw in a published benchmark.

Role
author (M.Sc. thesis) · Timeline: 2026 · Team: 1
Stack
PyTorch · TensorFlow · XGBoost · scikit-learn · pandas
Code
research code on GitHub

01Problem

Bike-sharing systems need hourly demand forecasts to place bikes where riders will be. The Seoul bike-sharing dataset has a published CUBIST benchmark that other work compares against.

The thesis question was which deep architecture forecasts hourly demand best, and whether the benchmark itself holds up.

02Constraints

  • Seven architectures compared under identical preprocessing and splits.
  • Time-series data: any leakage between train and test inflates the result.
  • Results must be reproducible by someone else.

03Architecture

04Key decisions

  • One preprocessing path for all models, so differences in R² come from the architecture and not from data handling.
  • Audit the benchmark before beating it. Reproducing the published pipeline exposed a data-leakage flaw that had inflated its reported results; the comparison uses the corrected version.
  • Multi-scale hybrid over a single architecture. TCN layers capture patterns at several time scales and the LSTM carries sequence state; the hybrid was the best performer.

target   hourly bike rentals

07What I'd do next

  • Probabilistic forecasts (prediction intervals), because operators plan around uncertainty and not a point estimate.
  • Test transfer to another city's bike-sharing data to see whether the multi-scale design generalises.
